Life Safety Protocol

Emergency Response & Mitigation Handbook

Heat Exhaustion Take Action
  • Heavy, profuse perspiration & cool, pale, clammy skin
  • Fast, weak pulse & muscle cramping
  • Dizziness, lightheadedness, or nausea
  • Normal or slightly elevated body temperature (<39°C)
Intervention: Move into AC immediately, loosen tight clothing, sip chilled water, and apply wet towels to neck.
Heat Stroke (Medical Emergency) Call 911 / 112
  • High body temperature (>40°C / 103°F)
  • Hot, red, dry or damp skin with NO active sweating
  • Rapid, pounding pulse & confusion or altered mental state
  • Slurred speech, seizures, or loss of consciousness
CRITICAL: Immediately phone local emergency services. Submerge in ice bath or soak garments in ice water while waiting.
pets Domestic Pets

Walk dogs only before 06:30 or after 21:00. Pavement exceeding 30°C causes severe pad blistering within 60 seconds. Never leave animals unattended in parked vehicles under any circumstance.

elderly Seniors & Vulnerable

Check on elderly neighbors twice daily. Aging sweat glands reduce thermoregulatory reflexes. Encourage non-alcoholic fluid intake and confirm functioning fans or proximity to cooling shelters.

child_care Infants & Children

Children generate more metabolic heat per pound than adults. Avoid stroller blanket covers which trap radiant thermal domes (raising carriage temps up to 8°C above ambient air).
